# macOS Menu-Bar App Main-Window Skeleton and Information Architecture

Last reviewed: 2026-08-12

This note records evidence on how established macOS menu-bar utilities organize a main window that mixes *content* (history, usage stats) with *configuration*, and on the history-list interaction conventions of voice-input peers. It informs restructuring Speaker's main window (currently five top tabs: 概览 Overview, 历史 History, 词典 Dictionary, 设置 Settings, 关于 About) under the constraints of extreme simplicity, a native look, and no inner section navigation inside a tab. App UI structures and HIG wording drift; recheck the cited primary sources before implementing.

## Decision summary

Keep the top `TabView` as Speaker's main-window skeleton, and do not adopt a System-Settings-style sidebar.

- Apple's HIG sanctions tab views for "closely related areas of content" and only warns above six tabs; Speaker has five. The sidebar pattern's own guidance concedes that "when space is limited … a more compact control such as a tab bar may provide a better navigation experience," and Speaker's window is small (min 720×560pt) with only five destinations — too few to justify a sidebar's cost. The apps that do use a sidebar (VoiceInk, Ice, AltTab, Wispr Flow Hub, Stats) carry 6–9 destinations or far richer per-pane content than Speaker. ([tab-views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views), [sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars))
- A single grouped-`Form` scroll per tab, with no inner section nav, is exactly what the closest peer does: VoiceInk's Settings pane is one `.grouped` `Form` with seven sections and no jump navigation, despite VoiceInk having a sidebar at the window level. Wispr Flow's Settings is likewise a sectioned scroll. The "no inner nav" constraint is peer-normal, not a compromise. ([VoiceInk SettingsView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/Settings/SettingsView.swift), [Wispr Flow app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android))
- Move About/version/check-for-updates out of the tab strip. The dominant convention is the app menu: "About" is the first app-menu item with a separator after it, and Sparkle's setup instructions put "Check for Updates…" in the application menu; VoiceInk and Maccy both follow this (VoiceInk adds Check for Updates via `CommandGroup(after: .appInfo)`). Settings-window About panes exist (Ice, AltTab) but are the minority pattern among apps with few destinations. Speaker is `LSUIElement`, but the app menu still appears while its window is focused, so the app-menu About panel remains viable — VoiceInk ships the same configuration with its "Hide Dock Icon" toggle. ([the-menu-bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar), [Sparkle setup](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/), [VoiceInk.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/VoiceInk.swift), [Maccy About.swift](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/Maccy/About.swift), [Speaker Info.plist](../../Resources/Info.plist))
- Move the privacy-boundary card and destructive local-data erasure from About into Settings as a "Privacy & Data" (or "Local Data") section. First-party precedent: Wispr Flow places "Delete history of all activity" under Settings → Data & Privacy with an explicit permanent-erasure warning, and VoiceInk keeps retention/cleanup controls in settings. Erasure is configuration of local data, not app metadata. ([Wispr Flow delete transcripts](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4465314211-delete-transcripts-and-history-in-wispr-flow), [VoiceInk SettingsView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/Settings/SettingsView.swift))
- For History polish, the peer conventions are: succinct rows (timestamp, duration, 1–2-line preview), a search field at the top, click-to-select with the full transcript in a detail area, hover-revealed quick actions (copy) and/or a right-click context menu (reprocess/delete) mirrored by visible controls, and a confirmation alert for non-undoable deletion naming the item count and "cannot be undone." HIG independently backs each of these (selection feedback, context menus mirrored in the main UI, alerts for uncommon non-undoable destructive actions). ([VoiceInk TranscriptionListItem.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/History/TranscriptionListItem.swift), [Wispr Flow delete transcripts](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4465314211-delete-transcripts-and-history-in-wispr-flow), [Superwhisper reprocess from history](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/transcribe-history), [lists-and-tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables), [context-menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus), [alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts))

## 1. Skeleton patterns in established menu-bar utilities

Six patterns appear in the surveyed apps, in rough order of prevalence for this app class:

**A. Paged preferences window (toolbar or tab strip switching panes).** Apple's own macOS settings guidance describes the canonical form: "a custom settings window contains a toolbar that includes buttons for switching between views — called panes — that each contain a group of related settings," with a noncustomizable toolbar that always indicates the active pane, the window title tracking the current pane, and the most recently viewed pane restored. ([settings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings)) Maccy implements exactly this via the `sindresorhus/Settings` package's `SettingsWindowController` with six panes (General, Storage, Appearance, Pins, Ignore, Advanced), each with a toolbar icon. ([Maccy AppState.swift](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/Maccy/Observables/AppState.swift)) CleanShot X uses a tabbed Preferences window (its changelog announces a "New 'Screenshots' tab in Preferences"). ([CleanShot changelog](https://cleanshot.com/changelog)) Speaker's current top `TabView` belongs to this family; HIG tab-view guidance explicitly permits up to six tabs and asks only that panes be "closely related" and self-contained. ([tab-views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views))

**B. System-Settings-style sidebar + detail.** Ice's settings window is a SwiftUI `NavigationSplitView` with a `List` sidebar of six items (General, Menu Bar Layout, Menu Bar Appearance, Hotkeys, Advanced, About), default size 900×625, min 825×500. ([Ice SettingsView.swift](https://github.com/jordanbaird/Ice/blob/main/Ice/Settings/SettingsView.swift), [Ice SettingsWindow.swift](https://github.com/jordanbaird/Ice/blob/main/Ice/Settings/SettingsWindow.swift), [Ice SettingsNavigationIdentifier.swift](https://github.com/jordanbaird/Ice/blob/main/Ice/Main/Navigation/NavigationIdentifiers/SettingsNavigationIdentifier.swift)) AltTab's settings window is an AppKit source-list sidebar with a search field, sectioned content panes, and an About tab. ([AltTab SettingsWindow.swift](https://github.com/lwouis/alt-tab-macos/blob/master/src/preferences/settings-window/SettingsWindow.swift), [AltTab tabs/AboutTab.swift](https://github.com/lwouis/alt-tab-macos/blob/master/src/preferences/settings-window/tabs/AboutTab.swift)) Stats uses an `NSSplitViewController` (180pt sidebar + content) in a fixed 720×480 window whose sidebar lists its modules plus Dashboard and application settings. ([Stats Settings.swift](https://github.com/exelban/stats/blob/master/Stats/Views/Settings.swift)) Superwhisper's settings window shows a three-item left sidebar — Configuration, Sound, History — with an "Advanced settings" push button at the bottom of the Configuration pane. ([Superwhisper settings screenshot](https://mintcdn.com/superwhisper/mIgwMoMMOZ7zvCCs/images/get-started/settings-advanced-001.png), [Superwhisper settings docs](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/settings))

**C. Custom sidebar mixing content and configuration in one window.** VoiceInk's main window is an HStack of a custom 220pt `AppSidebar` and a detail area; nine destinations are split into primary items at the top (Dashboard, Modes, Transcribe, History, Dictionary, AI Models, Audio) and secondary items pinned to the sidebar bottom (Settings, VoiceInk Pro). ([VoiceInk ContentView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/ContentView.swift), [VoiceInk AppSidebar.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/Sidebar/AppSidebar.swift)) Wispr Flow's desktop "Hub" uses the same arrangement: sidebar top-to-bottom Home (history + stats), Notifications, Dictionary, Snippets, Style, Scratchpad, with Settings and Help at the bottom. ([Wispr Flow app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android)) Note both pin Settings at the sidebar bottom despite HIG's caution to "avoid putting critical information or actions at the bottom of a sidebar" — that caution targets content/actions, and navigation items at the bottom of a short fixed list are a settled convention. ([sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars))

**D. Single scroll page per area (no inner navigation).** Inside whichever window-level skeleton, the pane itself is typically one grouped form: VoiceInk's Settings pane is a single `.grouped` `Form` with sections Shortcuts, Additional Shortcuts, Pasting, Interface, General, Backup, Diagnostics. ([VoiceInk SettingsView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/Settings/SettingsView.swift)) Wispr Flow's Settings page is a sectioned scroll (General, System, … then Account, Plans & Billing, Data & Privacy). ([Wispr Flow app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android)) This is the pattern Speaker's Settings tab already follows and the evidence supports keeping it at Speaker's scale (six sections).

**E. Separate windows per concern.** VoiceInk gives History both an inline main-window destination and a dedicated `NSWindow` (1250×750, min 1150×700) opened from its menu-bar menu's "History" item. ([VoiceInk HistoryWindowController.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/HistoryWindowController.swift), [VoiceInk MenuBarView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/MenuBarView.swift)) MacWhisper is document-centric: a homescreen plus per-transcript views. ([MacWhisper 11 release notes](https://impresskit.net/press-release/433ce979-0e7e-4f25-a6e9-a508035f0719))

**F. Menu-bar popover or menu as the primary surface, window only for settings.** Maccy's primary UI is a `FloatingPanel` popover with a footer menu (Preferences ⌘,, About, Quit); preferences open the paged window from pattern A. ([Maccy Footer.swift](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/Maccy/Observables/Footer.swift), [Maccy AppDelegate.swift](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/Maccy/AppDelegate.swift)) VoiceInk and Wispr Flow both make the menu-bar extra a plain *menu* (`menuBarExtraStyle(.menu)`; Wispr Flow's menu lists Open Wispr Flow, Paste last transcript, Shortcuts, Microphone, Languages, Help center), matching HIG's "display a menu — not a popover — when people click your menu bar extra." ([VoiceInk.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/VoiceInk.swift), [Wispr Flow app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android), [the-menu-bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar))

Net: top tabs/paged panes dominate when the window is small and destinations are few; sidebars appear when destinations reach roughly six or more, or when the window is large and content-heavy. Speaker (five destinations, min 720×560pt) sits in tab territory.

## 2. Voice-input peers

**VoiceInk (open source, verified from `main` source).** Main window: hidden-title-bar `Window` scene, custom sidebar + detail (pattern C); menu-bar extra is a classic menu (Toggle Recorder, mode/model pickers, Retry/Copy Last Transcription, History ⇧⌘H, Hide Dock Icon, Launch at Login, Settings ⌘,, Check for Updates, Quit). ([VoiceInk.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/VoiceInk.swift), [MenuBarView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/MenuBarView.swift)) Settings is one destination in the sidebar and internally one grouped form with no inner nav (pattern D). History lives as an inline sidebar destination (search field, card list, multi-select with a bottom action bar) and additionally as a separate large master-detail window. ([InlineHistoryView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/History/InlineHistoryView.swift), [HistoryWindowController.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/HistoryWindowController.swift)) About is not a window destination: the app uses the standard app-menu About, and adds Check for Updates to the app menu via `CommandGroup(after: .appInfo)`, plus a Check for Updates button inside Settings → General. ([VoiceInk.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/VoiceInk.swift), [SettingsView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/Settings/SettingsView.swift))

**Superwhisper (first-party docs and screenshots).** One settings window, pattern B with three sidebar items (Configuration, Sound, History); Configuration holds shortcut rows and an Application section containing "Update application — Check for Updates…", "Automatically check for updates", "Launch on login", "Error logging", with "Advanced settings" as a push navigation at the pane's bottom. ([settings screenshot](https://mintcdn.com/superwhisper/mIgwMoMMOZ7zvCCs/images/get-started/settings-advanced-001.png), [settings docs](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/settings)) History is a sidebar item inside that window and is also reachable from the menu bar and the mini recording window's context menu; it presents a search field over a left list, a center transcript/audio area, and a right inspector (Recording metadata, Configuration, Prompt) with a Voice/Segments/AI segmented control in the toolbar. ([interface-history](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-history), [history screenshot](https://mintcdn.com/superwhisper/hfjRgSCPJqwNcfX4/images/get-started/interface-history-001.png))

**Wispr Flow (first-party help center).** Desktop "Hub" window, pattern C: sidebar content-first, Settings/Help at the bottom; Home combines the full transcript history grouped by day with a stats card (streak, average WPM, total words) — i.e., Wispr Flow merges what Speaker splits into 概览 and 历史 into one Home. Settings is its own page with two groups (Settings: General, System, beta/experimental sections; Account: Account, Plans & Billing, Data & Privacy). On Mac it also keeps a full application menu bar (Wispr Flow, File, Edit, Dictation, My Voice, View, Help, Window). ([app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android))

**MacWhisper (first-party release notes/docs).** Document-centric transcription app rather than a menu-bar utility, though v11 added a menu-bar-only mode; its transcript view uses a collapsible sidebar for display settings, AI prompts, translations, transcript info, and speakers. ([release notes](https://impresskit.net/press-release/433ce979-0e7e-4f25-a6e9-a508035f0719))

Takeaways for Speaker: (1) every peer keeps *content* (history/stats) and *configuration* in one window; none uses a popover-only design for this mix. (2) Peers with few areas use flat sectioned scrolls; only VoiceInk and Superwhisper, both much larger in scope, pay for window-level sidebars or push navigation. (3) Stats+history merged into one Home is a proven simplification (Wispr Flow) worth considering for 概览/历史.

## 3. Apple HIG guidance

**Tab views vs sidebars.** "Use a tab view to present closely related areas of content… People expect each tab to display content that is in some way similar or related to the content in the other tabs." "Avoid providing more than six tabs in a tab view." On macOS, "the tabbed control appears on the top edge of the content area," and the guidance recommends insetting a tab view with a window-body margin. ([tab-views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views)) Sidebars "provide a broad, flat view of an app's information hierarchy" but "require a large amount of vertical and horizontal space. When space is limited or you want to devote more of the screen to other information or functionality, a more compact control such as a tab bar may provide a better navigation experience." On macOS, avoid critical content at the sidebar bottom and let people hide the sidebar. ([sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars))

**Settings windows.** Open via the app menu's Settings item and ⌘,; "typically, a custom settings window contains a toolbar that includes buttons for switching between views — called panes"; use "a noncustomizable toolbar that remains visible and always indicates the active toolbar button"; "update the window's title to reflect the currently visible pane" (or "App Name Settings" if single-pane); "restore the most recently viewed pane." More generally: "minimize the number of settings you offer," put "general, infrequently changed settings" in the settings area, and prefer task-specific options in context rather than in settings. ([settings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings))

**About panels.** In the app menu, "display the About menu item first. Include a separator after the About menu item so that it appears by itself in a group." The About item "displays the About window for your app, which includes copyright and version information," followed in the menu by Settings…. ([the-menu-bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar))

**Menu bar extras.** "A menu bar extra exposes app-specific functionality using an icon that appears in the menu bar when your app is running, even when it's not the frontmost app." "Display a menu — not a popover — when people click your menu bar extra. Unless the app functionality you want to expose is too complex for a menu, avoid presenting it in a popover." "Let people — not your app — decide whether to put your menu bar extra in the menu bar." ([the-menu-bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar))

## 4. About placement

The dominant convention is the **app menu**: About first in the app menu per HIG, with the standard About panel showing copyright/version, and Check for Updates as an app-menu item per Sparkle's setup instructions ("make a 'Check for Updates…' menu item in the application menu"). ([the-menu-bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar), [Sparkle setup](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/)) Survey results:

- **Maccy** — footer menu item "About" opens `orderFrontStandardAboutPanel` with custom credits/links; Check for Updates is a button in the General settings pane; Preferences opens from the footer with ⌘,. ([Maccy About.swift](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/Maccy/About.swift), [Footer.swift](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/Maccy/Observables/Footer.swift), [GeneralSettingsPane.swift](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/Maccy/Settings/GeneralSettingsPane.swift))
- **VoiceInk** — standard app-menu About plus Check for Updates injected into the app menu after the About group, and a second Check for Updates button in Settings → General. No About destination in the window. ([VoiceInk.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/VoiceInk.swift), [SettingsView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/Settings/SettingsView.swift))
- **Ice** — About is the last item of the settings-window sidebar (pattern B apps commonly absorb About). ([Ice SettingsNavigationIdentifier.swift](https://github.com/jordanbaird/Ice/blob/main/Ice/Main/Navigation/NavigationIdentifiers/SettingsNavigationIdentifier.swift))
- **AltTab** — About is one of the settings-window sidebar tabs. ([AltTab AboutTab.swift](https://github.com/lwouis/alt-tab-macos/blob/master/src/preferences/settings-window/tabs/AboutTab.swift))
- **Superwhisper** — no About surface in docs/screenshots; update controls live in Configuration → Application. ([settings screenshot](https://mintcdn.com/superwhisper/mIgwMoMMOZ7zvCCs/images/get-started/settings-advanced-001.png))
- **Wispr Flow** — no About page; account/plan/privacy live under Settings's Account group, Help at the sidebar bottom, and a full app menu bar exists on Mac (where an About item would conventionally sit). ([app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android))

For **privacy boundaries and destructive local-data erasure**, the only first-party convention found is Settings, not About: Wispr Flow's "Delete history of all activity" sits in Settings → Data & Privacy with a "permanently erases … cannot be undone" warning and a confirmation step; retention/cleanup toggles in VoiceInk live in its history-settings panel. ([Wispr Flow delete transcripts](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4465314211-delete-transcripts-and-history-in-wispr-flow), [VoiceInk InlineHistoryView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/History/InlineHistoryView.swift)) No surveyed app puts data-erasure controls in its About surface.

## 5. History / activity-list interaction conventions

**HIG.** Lists: provide selection feedback — "a table that helps people navigate through a hierarchy persistently highlights the selected row"; keep row text succinct and move large content to a detail view ("list item titles only, letting people choose an item to reveal its content in a detail view"). ([lists-and-tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables)) Context menus: few, highly relevant items; "always make context menu items available in the main interface, too"; put destructive items last; don't repeat keyboard shortcuts in context menus. ([context-menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus)) Destructive confirmation: "avoid displaying alerts for common, undoable actions, even when they're destructive… In comparison, when people take an uncommon destructive action that they can't undo, it's important to display an alert"; use a specific button title ("Erase," "Delete") rather than OK, always offer Cancel, and use the macOS caution symbol sparingly. ([alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts))

**Peer practice (transcription history specifically).**

- **Row anatomy.** VoiceInk's row: timestamp, duration badge, two-line transcript preview, rounded-card background, click to select. ([TranscriptionListItem.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/History/TranscriptionListItem.swift)) Wispr Flow groups transcripts by day (Today, Yesterday, date) with a search field at the top. ([app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android)) Superwhisper's list rows show a text preview, date, and duration under a "Search recordings" field. ([interface-history](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-history))
- **Hover-revealed actions vs always-visible.** Wispr Flow reveals a "⋯" button on hover per transcript (copy, flag, delete, etc.). ([delete transcripts](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4465314211-delete-transcripts-and-history-in-wispr-flow)) VoiceInk reveals a copy button on hover over the transcript detail (`hoverCopyButton`). ([InlineHistoryView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/History/InlineHistoryView.swift)) Hover-reveal is thus established practice in this product class for *per-item convenience actions*, with visible chrome (search, toolbars, selection bars) reserved for scope-wide actions.
- **Context menus.** Superwhisper: right-click a recording → "Process Again." ([transcribe-history](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/transcribe-history)) MacWhisper: right-click translations in the sidebar to remove them. ([release notes](https://impresskit.net/press-release/433ce979-0e7e-4f25-a6e9-a508035f0719))
- **Detail presentation.** The dominant form is selection → detail area, not expanding rows: VoiceInk's dedicated window is master-detail with optional right-side inspector panels; Superwhisper uses a three-pane layout (list / transcript+player / metadata inspector); MacWhisper opens a transcript view. No surveyed peer expands rows inline. ([TranscriptionHistoryView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/History/TranscriptionHistoryView.swift), [history screenshot](https://mintcdn.com/superwhisper/hfjRgSCPJqwNcfX4/images/get-started/interface-history-001.png), [MacWhisper release notes](https://impresskit.net/press-release/433ce979-0e7e-4f25-a6e9-a508035f0719))
- **Deletion and batch actions.** VoiceInk: multi-select via per-row checkboxes, a bottom selection bar with Analyze/Export/Delete and Select All, then an alert: "Delete Selected Items? … This action cannot be undone. Are you sure you want to delete N items?" with destructive Delete and Cancel. ([InlineHistoryView.swift](https://github.com/Beingpax/VoiceInk/blob/main/VoiceInk/Views/History/InlineHistoryView.swift)) Wispr Flow: hover ⋯ → Delete transcript → "Yes, delete it" confirmation; delete-all lives in Settings → Data & Privacy. ([delete transcripts](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/4465314211-delete-transcripts-and-history-in-wispr-flow)) Both match HIG's uncommon-non-undoable-destructive alert rule.

## Open questions / what we couldn't verify

- **Wispr Flow's About/update placement.** The help center documents no About screen or update UI; only the Mac app-menu bar's existence is documented. ([app map](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android))
- **Superwhisper's About surface** (if any) and whether its window is SwiftUI/AppKit; the sidebar structure is observed from official screenshots and docs, not source. ([settings screenshot](https://mintcdn.com/superwhisper/mIgwMoMMOZ7zvCCs/images/get-started/settings-advanced-001.png))
- **Closed-source utilities with rich settings** (Bartender, iStat Menus, One Switch) were not surveyed; their inclusion could shift the pattern frequencies in section 1, though not the HIG or peer findings.
- **Whether hover-revealed per-row actions have an explicit HIG endorsement** — HIG documents context menus and selection feedback but says nothing about hover chrome on macOS list rows; the practice is evidenced only by peer apps (Wispr Flow, VoiceInk).
- **Exact window metrics** for Wispr Flow Hub and Superwhisper's window (sizes, min constraints) are not published in their docs.
